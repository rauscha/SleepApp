You find yourself standing at the edge of a small village, just where the road curves gently down toward the sea. It is evening, and the last of the light has gone from the sky without any particular announcement. The sky above you is a deep, soft grey, neither dark nor light, just that quiet middle color that comes after sunset when the world has finished with the day and has not yet decided what to dream. The fog is already moving in from the water, slow and unhurried, drifting between the low stone buildings like something that has always lived here, like something that belongs.

You begin to walk. Your footsteps fall softly on the damp cobblestones, and each step makes a small, quiet sound, a sound like a word whispered in a language you almost understand. The stones are dark with moisture and they catch the faint light of the lamps overhead, throwing it back up at you in long, blurred reflections. The cobblestones are uneven in a gentle way, worn smooth by many years and many feet, and you feel each small rise and dip through the soles of your shoes. It is an old road. It has been walked many times before.

[softly] The village is very quiet tonight.

Every shop along the narrow street is closed. The windows are dark, or nearly dark, just the faint inner shadow of shelves and counters visible through the glass. A small bakery sits to your left, its window empty of the day's bread, a few wicker baskets stacked inside against the wall. The door is closed. A wooden sign above it turns very slightly in the easiest of breezes, barely moving, just enough to tell you the air is here, the air is present, the air is moving through.

You pass a small hardware shop, its window crowded with quiet objects. Coils of rope. Brass fittings. A row of rubber boots standing together in the dark. Everything is motionless and settled. Everything has been exactly where it is for a long, long time. You do not need anything from any of these shops. You are only walking. You are only here to walk.

The fog moves across the street in long, slow ribbons. It does not hurry. It takes its time curling around the corner of a low stone wall, then stretching itself across the width of the road, then thinning again where one of the lamps holds it back with a soft circle of amber light. The lamps are old-fashioned, mounted on iron brackets that curve out from the stone buildings, and they give off a warm, dim glow that does not reach very far. Each lamp lights only a small portion of the world. The rest is left to the fog and the grey.

[softly] It is enough light. It is exactly enough.

You walk beneath one of the lamps and feel the faint warmth of it on your face, and then you step past it and the warmth fades and the cool air returns. The cool air is pleasant. It smells of salt and wet stone and something else underneath, something old and green, like seaweed drying slowly somewhere nearby. You breathe in. You breathe out. The air moves through you easily and your shoulders drop a little lower than they were before.

The street curves slightly to the right and you follow it without thinking, your feet knowing the way on their own. Ahead of you the road widens just a little into what might once have been a small square, though there is nothing in it now but a stone bench and a low iron railing and a lamp on a post at the center. The lamp here is slightly brighter than the others, or perhaps it only seems that way because the fog has gathered around it and holds the light suspended, a soft globe of amber floating in the grey. You walk toward it slowly.

The bench is wet. You do not sit down. You stand near it for a moment, looking at it, and then you look up and out toward where the sea must be, though you cannot see it from here. You can hear it, though. The waves are very distant, a low continuous sound, a sound like breathing, like the sea itself is breathing slowly in and out, in and out. The sound comes and goes and comes again in a rhythm that is older than the village, older than the road, older than the lamps.

[softly] You listen to it for a long moment.

Then you walk on. Past the square, past the iron railing, down a narrower lane that tilts gently toward the water. The buildings here are smaller, older, their stone walls thick and pale in the foglight. The windows are set deep in the walls, like eyes half-closed, and the glass in them is old and slightly wavy, so that the reflection of the lamplight in each pane is stretched and soft, a small smear of warmth in the darkness. You pass one window and then another and then a third, each one the same, each one a small private darkness behind wavy old glass.

The lane narrows further and the buildings lean in slightly from both sides, not uncomfortably, just close, just near, as though the village is gently holding the lane between its old stone hands. The cobblestones here are darker and wetter than on the main street, and your footsteps are quieter, softer, the sound absorbed by the fog. You can barely hear yourself walking. You can only feel it, the slight give and rise of the uneven stones, the faint cool dampness rising up through your soles.

A cat is sitting on a low wall to your right. It is a grey cat, or perhaps a dark one, it is difficult to say in this light and fog, and it watches you pass without moving, without any alarm, with the complete indifference of a cat who has watched many people walk this lane and found them all equally unremarkable. Its eyes catch the lamplight for a moment, two small calm reflections, and then you have walked past and it is behind you.

[softly] Everything is calm. Everything is settled.

At the end of the lane the buildings fall away and you find yourself on a small promenade, a wide flat path of stone that runs along the edge of the village where the village meets the sea. There is a low wall along the outer edge, knee-high, built from the same pale stone as everything else here, and beyond it the ground drops away and there is only fog and the sound of water. You cannot see the sea itself. You can only hear it, closer now, a little louder, the long slow draw of waves pulling back over stone and pebble, and then the softer sound of them returning.

[pause]

You rest your hands on the top of the wall. The stone is cool and slightly rough and very solid under your palms. You stand there and listen. The fog moves around you, shifting and resettling, and in the fog the lamp at the far end of the promenade is a faint warm blur, barely visible, just a suggestion of light. The lamp nearest to you throws a small circle of amber down onto the wet stone at your feet. Beyond that circle everything is soft and indistinct.

The sound of the waves is very steady. It does not change. It does not hurry or slow. It has been doing this all day while the village went about its business, all evening while the shops closed and the lights went out one by one, and it will continue doing this through the whole of the night, through every hour of the dark, unhurried and certain.

[softly] You find this very easy to listen to.

You turn and walk slowly along the promenade, following the curve of the wall. Your footsteps are the only footsteps. There is no one else here. The whole village seems to be sleeping, every door closed, every window dark or nearly dark, the people inside already in their beds, already in their own quiet depths. You are the only one still out here in the fog and the lamp glow, and even you are barely here. You are walking very slowly now. You are barely moving.

[pause]

On your left a row of small buildings faces the sea, their fronts flush with the promenade wall. A fisherman's supply shop, shuttered completely, the metal shutter pulled down and padlocked. A small café, its sign unlit, its chairs invisible inside. A narrow office of some kind, the name on the glass too faint to read in this light, the letters worn and indistinct. You walk past all of them without stopping. You are not looking for anything. You are only walking. You are only here to be here.

The fog is thicker now than when you started. It has come in steadily while you have been walking, rolling in off the water in long quiet waves of its own, and the lamps along the promenade are softening into it, each one a little more blurred than the last. The furthest lamps are invisible. They are only a faint brightening in the grey, a suggestion that somewhere out there there is light, that somewhere the dark is held back slightly.

[softly] It is enough. It is more than enough.

You reach the far end of the promenade where a set of wide stone steps leads back up into the village, and you stop at the bottom of them and look up. The steps are old and worn at their centers, hollowed gently by years of feet going up and down, and they disappear after four or five of them into the fog and the dark, the rest only implied. You put one foot on the first step and then the second and then the third and you climb slowly, feeling the slight effort of it, the soft protest of muscles that have been walking for a while now and are ready to stop.

[pause]

At the top of the steps you are on another narrow street, parallel to the one you started on, running behind the promenade buildings. The lamp here is very dim, more a feeling than a light, and the fog has settled so thickly between the buildings that the far end of the street is invisible, the whole world reduced to this small section of wet stone and grey air and the faint amber above you. You stand in it for a moment. You are not cold. You are the right temperature. The air is cool on your face and your hands but the rest of you is warm, the warmth from walking still in your chest and your legs.

You begin to walk again, very slowly now, back in the direction of where you started, though you are not in any hurry to get there. The sound of the sea is behind you, a little further now, the waves still audible but softer, muffled by the buildings and the fog, a low continuous murmur that seems to come from everywhere at once. You walk and you listen and the sound goes with you.

[softly] The cobblestones glisten in the lamplight.

Somewhere ahead and to your left, a very faint sound. A clock somewhere inside one of these buildings, striking the hour with soft, low tones. You count without meaning to. One. Two. Three. And then it is done and the silence comes back, fuller than before, the silence that follows a small sound always fuller than the silence before it. You walk through it. Your footsteps are soft. The fog is all around you.

[pause]

The buildings on either side of you are old and quiet and completely still. Their walls are thick and the windows are small and the lives inside them are very far away right now, tucked in and settled and breathing slowly. Everything breathing slowly. The fog breathing slowly across the street. The sea breathing slowly somewhere behind you. Your own breath, which has been slowing for some time now, which has been taking its time, which has been following the rhythm of the water without you asking it to.

You pass a doorway set back slightly from the street, its dark wood door closed, a small iron knocker on it in the shape of something you cannot quite make out in this light. A fish, perhaps. Or a crescent. You do not stop to look. You keep walking. The door is closed. Everything is closed. Everything is resting.

[softly] The fog settles around you like something soft.

The lane you are on bends gently to the right and you follow it and find yourself back near where you started, the road that curves down toward the sea, the place where the last light left the sky. Everything looks the same as it did, the same damp cobblestones, the same dim lamps, the same pale stone walls with their dark windows. And yet everything feels different. The village feels closer now, more familiar, as though in walking through it you have become part of it for tonight, just one more quiet thing moving slowly through the fog.

[pause]

You slow down. Your footsteps slow with you. The cobblestones beneath your feet are so familiar now, the rise and fall of them, the slight unevenness that you have learned to walk with, that you move over without thinking. The lamps above you are very soft. The fog has softened them further. Each lamp is barely there, barely a lamp anymore, more like a warmth, more like a feeling of warmth, something amber and low and far away.

The sound of the waves is still there. You have been hearing it for so long now that it no longer sounds like anything external. It has become part of the quiet, part of the texture of this place, part of the rhythm of your own breathing and your own heart. In and out. Back and forth. The same as it has always been. The same as it will be.

[softly] You are walking very slowly now.

The street ahead of you is long and dim and the fog has filled it completely so that there is only a short stretch of glistening stone and then grey, only the nearest lamp and its small circle of warmth and then the rest dissolving into the soft and formless dark. You walk toward the lamp. You pass beneath it. The warmth touches your face for a moment and then falls behind you.

[pause]

The next lamp is further. Its light is very faint from here, a small warm blur in all that grey, a small warm blur getting slowly closer as you walk toward it. Your feet find the cobblestones without effort. Your body knows how to do this. Your body has known how to do this for a long time. You just walk. You just put one foot and then the other and the stones receive each step softly and the fog moves around you as you pass and the waves go on and on in their slow unhurried way far below and behind you.

Everything is very still. The village is asleep. The fog is in every lane and alley, resting against every door and window, lying along every rooftop in long soft layers. The lamps burn on in it, small and warm, not fighting the dark, just being there in it, just giving off their small warmth into the grey without insisting on anything.

[softly] You are almost at the next lamp now.
