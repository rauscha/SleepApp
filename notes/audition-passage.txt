The train rocks just slightly. Not enough to disturb you. Just enough to remind you that you are moving, that you are being carried, that there is nothing required of you right now except to let yourself be carried. The rails beneath you produce a sound that is less a sound and more a rhythm, a low, steady conversation between metal and motion. [softly] It goes on and on, the same and the same, a little slower than you expect, a little quieter than you remember.

There is heat in the cabin. It comes from somewhere low and constant, a warmth that fills the space around your feet and rises slowly, gradually, so that by the time it reaches your shoulders it is just a gentle heaviness, the kind that asks nothing of you. You are dressed warmly. Your coat is folded on the seat beside you or perhaps you are still wearing it, just loosened, the collar turned down, the sleeves soft against your arms. It does not matter. You are warm. [pause]

There are a few other passengers in the car. Not many. An older man two rows ahead, his head turned toward his own dark window, very still. A woman near the far end reading something, though the pages turn slowly, less and less often. No one is speaking. There is nothing to speak about. The train is the only conversation and it is saying the same thing it has been saying for hours, [softly] steady, steady, steady.

At the next station the train slows. You feel it before you hear it, a gradual easing, the rhythm pulling back like a long slow breath. The station is small — a platform, a single lamp on a post, a wooden bench with snow on the seat. No one is waiting. The doors open and for a moment cool air moves through the car, not sharp, just fresh, carrying the smell of cold and pine and something faint beneath that, wood smoke from somewhere you cannot see. Then the doors close again. The train moves. [pause]
